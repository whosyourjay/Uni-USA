Top 10: the remainder is a 2021 event, not a trend

Six straight years under 25 students, then 1,952. Averaging 2014–2023 folds that cliff into every mean; a 2014–2019 window would leave the top ten essentially fully scored. The cohort also thins as it breaks — all ten reported through 2020, seven in 2021 — so part of the drop is schools going quiet rather than students arriving untested.

For context: every blank sits below rank 1,223

Share of each rank band with no submitter count in any of the ten years. Of the 899 blanks, 518 are open-admission and 604 report no admission-test policy at all — they are not selective schools that quietly stopped reporting.

The top ten are measured, not estimated

Caltech is the one to watch: it went test-blind, stopped reporting after 2020, and its mean now rests on seven pre-2021 years. Treating those missing years as unreported rather than unscored is what keeps its estimate at 3%.

Nine schools in the top 100 estimate over a quarter unscored

The pattern is sparse reporting, not admissions philosophy. Trinity and Connecticut College report in 6 of 10 years, Holy Cross and Union in 5 — and the years they do report already carry a large unscored share. IPEDS records class rank as "considered but not required" at all nine, so it cannot say what replaced the tests.
